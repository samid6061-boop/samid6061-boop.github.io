Urdu SEO: Urdu, Roman Urdu or English? A keyword identity crisis

Picture three people, all looking for the same thing. One types it in Urdu script, one in Roman Urdu, one in English. Your keyword list, meanwhile, only invited one of them to the party. Let's fix the guest list.

Meet the three searchers

The English searcher
cheap laptop Pakistan

Efficient, polite, and the one every keyword tool knows by name.

The Roman Urdu searcher
sasta laptop Pakistan

Types the way they talk. Spelling is a creative decision, and nobody calls an inspector.

The Urdu script searcher
سستا لیپ ٹاپ

Same intent, different alphabet, and possibly no one on your team checking for them.

These three phrases are illustrations, not search-volume data.

Why your keyword tool might be looking the wrong way

Several SEO guides written for Pakistan say the same thing: people search in English, in Urdu script, and in Roman Urdu, and the usual tools tend to undercount the last two. One reason is that Roman Urdu has no single agreed spelling, so the same word can be typed several ways. A tool built around tidy English phrases is not going to enjoy that.

I can't give you a percentage for how your audience splits, and be wary of anyone who offers one without your data. What I can give you is a way to find out.

The 15-minute test (free, no subscription, no crying)

1. Type your topic three ways

Search it in Google in English, Roman Urdu, and Urdu script. Same topic, three searches.

2. Read the suggestions

Google's autocomplete and "People also ask" boxes show phrasing people actually use. Write down what appears for each version.

3. Look at who ranks

If the Roman Urdu or Urdu results look thin, that may be a gap. If they're packed, you've learned something cheaply.

4. Check your own data

Once your site has a few weeks of traffic, the Queries report in Google Search Console shows exactly what people typed to find you. It beats any guess, including mine.

5. Ask real people

Local Facebook groups and forums show how people phrase things when they aren't thinking about SEO at all.

What not to do

Do not stuff all three versions into one title. A headline that reads "cheap laptop sasta laptop سستا لیپ ٹاپ" looks like a ransom note, and readers will treat it accordingly. Pick a main phrase for each page and write it naturally.

And sometimes the answer is just English

Honest moment: Urdu content isn't a requirement for ranking in Pakistan. Plenty of businesses do fine in English. Urdu and Roman Urdu pages are worth the effort when your research shows real demand in those forms, or when your readers simply prefer them.

If you do go Urdu script

  • Use real Unicode text, not images and not transliteration passed off as Urdu. Text inside an image can't be searched.
  • If you publish separate English and Urdu versions of a page, tell Google which is which using hreflang.
  • Have a native reader check it. See how to hire an Urdu content writer for what to look for.

The short version: don't guess your audience's script, test it. Fifteen minutes of searching beats an afternoon of arguing about it.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use Roman Urdu keywords?

Test first. Several Pakistani SEO guides recommend including them where your research shows people actually search that way. Check autocomplete, search results, and your own Search Console queries before committing.

Do I need Urdu content to rank in Pakistan?

No. English-only sites can rank well. Urdu or Roman Urdu content matters when it matches how your audience searches and reads, so it's a choice to make with evidence, not a rule.
