YouTube SEO for Shorts: what actually gets a Short seen

Most YouTube SEO advice was written for long videos, where a viewer reads a title and clicks. Shorts don't work that way. Here's what changes, what doesn't, and what I've seen on my own channel.

A Short is found in two places, not one

A long video is mostly found because someone searched for it or clicked a thumbnail. A Short is mostly found in the Shorts feed, where it simply starts playing. There is no thumbnail to click and no title to read first. The viewer's only decision is whether to keep watching or swipe away.

Shorts can also show up in YouTube search and in Google results, which is where titles and descriptions still do real work. So Shorts SEO has two jobs: win the feed, then be easy to find by search.

The feed comes first: viewed vs. swiped away

In YouTube Studio, your Shorts analytics show a "viewed vs. swiped away" number. It tells you how often people who were shown your Short watched it instead of scrolling past. Practitioners widely treat it as the most important Shorts signal, alongside how much of the video people watch (average percentage viewed).

One more thing worth knowing: since March 2025, a Short's view count goes up the moment it starts playing. That makes raw views a weaker signal than they used to be, and makes the two numbers above more useful than the headline count.

The practical result is that the first one or two seconds matter more than any keyword. If the opening doesn't promise something specific, the rest of your optimization never gets a chance to work.

What metadata still does

☐ Title: put the words a viewer would actually type near the start, and keep it short enough not to be cut off. A title that matches a real search beats a clever one.

☐ Description: two or three plain sentences, with your main phrase in the first one. Long descriptions add little for Shorts. A link to a related longer video is a better use of the space.

☐ Hashtags: a few relevant ones (three to five), not dozens. They help categorize the topic but are a secondary signal. #Shorts is optional, since YouTube identifies a Short by its format.

☐ On-screen and spoken words: captions and text on screen give YouTube more to understand about the topic, and many people watch without sound.

What I've seen on my own channel

I run The Pavilion Report, a sports channel where many of my uploads are vertical Shorts. I can't tell you which single factor caused any result, and nobody honestly can. But the pattern on my channel has been consistent:

  • In one 48-hour stretch, the channel pulled in over 25,000 views, driven by videos about a specific event people were actively following: the PSL players' auction.
  • Another stretch, during the ICC Men's T20 World Cup, brought over 100,000 views in 48 hours, with a single video accounting for roughly 70,000 of them.
  • When I moved into FIFA World Cup content, views held above 40,000 a week for several weeks. The approach worked on a new topic, not just cricket.

The common thread: each spike came from content built around something people were already looking for at that moment, uploaded while the interest was live. Timing and topic did more than any tag.

A simple routine for every Short

  1. Pick a topic people are searching for right now, not one you'd like them to search for.
  2. Write the opening line first. If it doesn't create a reason to stay, rewrite it.
  3. Add a title with the key phrase early, a short description, and a few hashtags.
  4. After a few days, check viewed vs. swiped away. If it's weak, the hook is the problem, not the metadata.
  5. Reuse what worked: same format, new topic.

Frequently asked questions

Does SEO work for YouTube Shorts?

Yes, but differently from long-form video. Most Shorts views come from the Shorts feed, where viewer behavior (whether people watch or swipe away) matters most. Titles, descriptions, and hashtags still matter for search and for helping YouTube understand the topic.

Do hashtags matter for YouTube Shorts?

They are a minor, secondary signal. A few relevant hashtags help categorize a Short, but a clear title and a good opening matter far more than a long list of tags.
