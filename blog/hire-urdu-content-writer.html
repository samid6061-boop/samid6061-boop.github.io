How to hire an Urdu content writer: 7 checks before you pay

Search for an Urdu content writer and you'll find marketplace profiles, agency pages, and job boards. What you won't find is much help in telling a good writer from a weak one. Here are the checks I'd run if I were the one hiring.

The 7 checks

1Decide which script your readers actually use

Urdu content online comes in more than one form: Urdu script, Roman Urdu, and plain English. Before you hire anyone, decide which one your audience reads and searches in. Look at your own analytics or search data rather than assuming, because the right writer for Urdu script is not automatically the right one for Roman Urdu.

2Ask for published Urdu samples, not just a profile

A profile tells you what someone says they can do. A link to published Urdu writing shows you what they've done. Ask for pieces that were written in Urdu from the start, and read them yourself, or have a native reader do it.

3Check that the tone matches your readers

Urdu has formal, literary registers and plain conversational ones. A news column, a product page, and a social post each need a different voice. If every sample you're shown sounds the same, the writer probably has one register.

4Insist on real, selectable Urdu text

Ask for the text delivered as proper Unicode text you can copy, not as images, screenshots, or files that turn into garbled characters when pasted. Text inside an image can't be searched or reliably indexed, and that defeats the point of publishing for search.

5Ask who proofreads it

Automated spell-checking for Urdu is generally less dependable than it is for English, so a human proofreading pass matters more. A writer who has a clear answer here has usually thought about quality.

6Pay for a small test piece first

Before a large order, commission a short paid piece on your own topic. It costs little, and it tells you more than any portfolio about whether the writer understands your audience and follows a brief.

7Ask how the text is made

Machine-translated and unedited AI Urdu often reads stiff, because it was built from English phrasing instead of how an Urdu reader would put it. Ask whether the writer works from scratch in Urdu, and how any tools are used and edited.

What about price?

Rates vary widely with experience, topic, and how much research a piece needs, so I won't quote a single number. What I'd compare is what's included: research, proofreading, and revisions. A very low quote can be a sign of machine translation or copied text, so it's worth asking how the price is possible. Where to look

Marketplaces such as Fiverr and Upwork, agencies, and independent writers with published bylines can all work. Where you find someone matters much less than whether they pass the seven checks above.

A quick way to use this list: ask every candidate the same questions, in writing, and compare the answers side by side. The differences usually show up fast.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a good Urdu content writer?

Look for published Urdu work, ask for a small paid test on your own topic, and check script, tone, text format, and proofreading before committing to a larger order.

Is an Urdu content writer the same as a translator?

No. A translator converts existing text faithfully, while a content writer creates original content in Urdu for a specific audience. The difference is explained in bilingual content writer vs. translator.
