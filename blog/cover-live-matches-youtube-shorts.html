How I cover live matches on YouTube Shorts with one laptop and AI

People ask how one person keeps up with live cricket and football. The honest answer is not a secret tool. It's a routine I repeat every match, a few AI tools, and the habit of fixing one mistake at a time. Here's the routine.

The setup: one laptop, a few AI tools

Everything below happens on a single laptop. These are the tools I use, and what I use each one for:

Writing

ChatGPT for instant, well-rounded answers. Claude for structured output: when a task doesn't need much explaining, it can return the whole package in one response, such as titles and descriptions.

Research and stats

Perplexity for quick, relevant day-to-day snippets. Gemini's deep research mode when I need something more in-depth and well-rounded.

Graphics

ChatGPT's image generator, and Google's Gemini image models, including the one nicknamed Nano Banana.

One caution for any AI tool: they can get numbers wrong. For sports stats, check the figures against an official source before you post.

Match day, step by step

3 hours beforeResearch and planning

Keyword research, setting the visual direction for the graphics, and checking that my editing tools are ready. By the time the match starts, the thinking is already done.

Then I cover the match live, in phases:

Phase 1Toss and playing XI

The first update goes out as soon as the teams are known.

Phase 2First-innings highlights

A summary of how the first innings went.

Phase 3Final-innings highlights

The closing update once the result is clear.

If it's a tournamentPoints table

When the match is part of a tournament, I add an updated points table.

The same routine carries over to FIFA coverage and other sports. The sport changes, the routine doesn't.

How long one Short takes

Most of my videos are vertical 9:16 Shorts. From planning to post-production, a single-frame Short takes me about 30 minutes. That time is only realistic because the research and the visual direction were settled before the match started.

Mistakes I fixed along the way

I made plenty of mistakes. What helped was fixing them one at a time instead of changing everything at once:

  • Flat graphics. My early graphics looked flat. I started including player faces.
  • Layout. I moved the graphics into a hero-section style layout, and then added text, percentages, deliberate image placement, and a defined colour palette.
  • Titles. At the start I tried different styles to lift click-through rates: spicy, news-focused, informational, emotional, and nostalgic.

After every series, I look at what worked and improve the next one. That loop is the whole method.

What the numbers looked like

These figures come from my own YouTube Studio analytics: over 25,000 views in a single day during PSL players' auction coverage, over 100,000 views in 48 hours during the ICC Men's T20 World Cup, and more than 560,000 views across that tournament's coverage. When I moved into FIFA World Cup content, views held above 40,000 a week for several weeks. I can't tell you which single change caused which result. I can tell you the routine stayed the same while the graphics and titles improved. The wider story is on my homepage, in My Creative Work.

The takeaway: consistency is the part nobody can shortcut. The same routine, every match, with one thing improved after each series.
