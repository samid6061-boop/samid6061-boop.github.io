Video SEO for small businesses: how to make short videos actually get found

A well-made video that nobody searches into is a well-made video that doesn't work. Most small businesses spend their whole budget on production and none on the five-minute setup that decides whether the video is findable at all.

Why videos disappear even when the content is good

Search engines and platforms like YouTube can't watch your video the way a person can. They rely almost entirely on the text around it — the title, description, captions, and file metadata — to understand what it's about and who to show it to. Skip that step, and even a great video sits with near-zero views, not because it's bad, but because nothing told the algorithm it exists.

The five things that actually move the needle

☐ Title with the actual search term in it, not just a clever line. "5 Signs Your Roof Needs Repair" outperforms "Is Your Roof Trying to Tell You Something?" every time — save the clever line for the hook inside the video.

☐ A real description, not a one-liner. 150+ words summarizing what the video covers, written for a human, with your target phrase used naturally once or twice near the start.

☐ Captions or a transcript, always. This is the single most skipped step. Captions give search engines the entire spoken content to index — and most short-form tools now generate them automatically, so there's no excuse to skip it.

☐ A custom thumbnail, not an auto-generated frame. Click-through rate is itself a ranking signal on most platforms — a thumbnail with clear text or a real face outperforms a random paused frame almost every time.

☐ The file name before you even upload it. "roof-repair-signs-2026.mp4" gives search engines one more data point than "IMG_4821.mp4" — a small thing that costs nothing to fix.

Where AI-assisted production changes the economics

Doing this properly used to mean an editor's time for scripting, captioning, and thumbnail design on top of the shoot itself. AI-assisted workflows collapse most of that: auto-generated captions that only need a light human edit pass, script drafts built around the actual search term instead of written blind, and thumbnail variations generated in minutes instead of hours. The production speeds up; the SEO discipline doesn't have to get skipped to make that happen.

A quick test before you publish

Type your video's title into the search bar of whatever platform it's going on. If ten other videos with a similar title and a bigger channel come up, either narrow the title to something more specific, or accept that you're competing at a disadvantage on that exact phrase. Specific, slightly narrower titles consistently outperform broad ones for smaller channels — "how to fix a leaking kitchen tap" over "plumbing tips."
